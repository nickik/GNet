# Chat Archive Overview

This folder preserves project design conversations that provide historical context for decisions documented elsewhere in the GNet knowledge base.

## [[Cosx vs Twiter Pair GNet Chat]]

Discussion of early GNet bootstrap, discovery, addressing, and terminal networking. It develops the idea that link-local broadcast should be minimal: a host uses a generic `DISCOVER` protocol to find foundational infrastructure such as a router, receives directed replies and network configuration, then uses routed directory services for higher-level discovery. The discussion also separates physical hub attachment numbers from logical routed addresses, keeps hubs deliberately dumb, considers ROM-booted GNet terminals and terminal-session services, and explores smart-card-based user authentication with directory/authentication services rather than embedding identity in the link layer.

## [[GNet Layers 2-4 and DEC Compatibility Strategy Chat]]

Large design discussion that substantially refines the GNet stack and connects it to the broader alternate-DEC strategy. At Layer 2 it converges on a 3 Mb/s SmartHub office LAN using telephone-style star wiring, a separate control pair for request/grant arbitration, no MAC-style source/destination addresses, centralized two-level priority, minimal framing/CRC, and an L2 type distinction between full Global GNet and compact 8-bit Local GNet. DDCMP is treated as useful inspiration for framing and point-to-point links but too heavy for the SmartHub access LAN. Layer 3 remains a small routed datagram layer with 48-bit global addresses, while local traffic can use a short non-routable form. Layer 4 becomes tunnel-first: a 64-bit Tunnel ID identifies a mobile connection, a capability-like Reset ID is only required for rebind/close/reset, a tunnel binds its Service ID once, and 8-bit Stream IDs multiplex reliable, datagram, sequenced-datagram, and future encrypted/compressed profiles. The default reliable stream retains TCP-like sequence/ACK/retransmission semantics plus compact credit flow control; credit does not replace ACKs.

The same discussion develops the commercial strategy around GNet: cheap nearly configuration-free floor hubs are separated from router, file/print, terminal-server, and PBX service appliances; the attachment/wire standard is open and third-party NICs are allowed after conformance testing, while higher-level DEC services may remain proprietary. It estimates that a successful GNet ecosystem could make DEC a major LAN/router/office-telephony player alongside a stronger RISC-VAX workstation/server business.

The latter part develops a dedicated IBM-compatibility strategy for DEC's commercial organization. Instead of merely supporting RPG/COBOL, DEC should sell explicit `DECSystem/34`, `DECSystem/36`, and `DECSystem/38` migration/compatibility systems backed by DEC field support, with /36 as the strongest initial target and /38 requiring deeper MI, CPF/CL, object-model, and database compatibility. For IBM System/360/370-class compatibility, the preferred acquisition path is to capture Itel's Advanced Systems business around 1979, then distressed Magnuson assets and selected IPL/Cambex/StorageTek expertise; NAS is the fallback if Itel is missed, while Amdahl is better treated as a high-end strategic partner/equity relationship because of Fujitsu. The end-state is not preservation of many clone hardware lines, but supported IBM operating environments virtualized on the common RISC/ECL DEC architecture, using acquired compatibility test suites, channel/I/O expertise, customer bases, and field-service organizations as the bridge.

## [[Zero-Configuration Routing and Address Delegation Chat]]

Discussion that refines GNet router bootstrap into a state-driven, recursive zero-configuration model. It replaces conventional `HELLO` semantics with a child-to-parent `STATUS` report describing local interfaces, current link state, address demand/capacity, delegated ranges, capabilities, and optional load/performance hints. The parent answers with `DELEGATION`, assigning address capacity and reporting relevant upstream information. Routers resend `STATUS` after meaningful events and at randomized intervals, so the next report reflects the state created by the previous delegation. The model assumes no burned-in router ID and no topology preconfiguration: router identity is operational/topological rather than hardware-assigned, and delegated address space can be recursively re-delegated downstream without NAT.

The discussion compares this mechanism with DHCPv6 Prefix Delegation, IPv6 Router Advertisements, LLDP/CDP, IS-IS/OSPF/RPL, and BGP. Its durable design point is to combine address delegation and router-state exchange into a very small local control mechanism rather than requiring separate manually configured systems. It also records the move toward human-facing hierarchical address terminology `Top`, `Org`, and `Division`, with decimal notation and link-local shorthand preferred over raw hexadecimal. A GDP header printed in this archived conversation is explicitly marked superseded by later GNet decisions, including the current baseline 32-bit flit with **2-bit VCID + 30 carried bits**, with VC4 (`4 + 28`) reserved as a future negotiable profile, and the decision that GDP itself carries no integrity checksum.

## [[Service Names RPC DNS and Internet Plumbing 2026-09-03]]

Discussion of human-readable service selection and the higher layers above GDP. It proposes replacing user-visible numeric ports with short service names such as `ssh`, `sysinfo`, and `dns`, carried only during GTS connection/stream setup and then represented by established tunnel/stream state. This makes service discovery and a standard RPC/reflection layer natural: a host can describe available interfaces and standard services without embedding application semantics into GDP.

The discussion places native name resolution at Layer 7 over GTS/RPC — analogous in architectural role to DNS-over-HTTP, but without requiring HTTP — and surveys the operational functions a global GNet needs beyond application protocols: bootstrap/address delegation, interior and exterior routing, control/error reporting, multicast, time, security, management, and naming. It also records a proposed 27-bit shared Flow/Tunnel identifier as historical exploration; this conflicts with the current minimal GDP baseline and therefore remains an unresolved transport-layer optimization rather than a GDP field.

The final part clarifies the wormhole-style link model. DLP VCIDs, receiver credits, grants and on/off backpressure are hop-local mechanisms for safe cut-through forwarding and scheduling. They do **not** replace end-to-end GTS sequencing, ACK/retransmission, integrity or congestion responsibilities. This corrects an overreach in the exploratory discussion and matches the current separation between DLP, GDP and GTS.

## [[DECnet Phase I and II Historical Reference 2026-09-03]]

Historical reference discussion on the architecture and wire behavior of **DECnet Phase I and Phase II**, preserved for comparison with GNet rather than as a GNet specification. Phase I is characterized as an essentially two-node point-to-point system built around reliable DDCMP links. Phase II expands to small multi-node networks but still lacks general multi-hop routing; routing and a routed numeric addressing architecture arrive later. The discussion details Phase II's use of human-readable node names plus a numeric `NODEADDR` exchanged during node initialization, and maps familiar IP concepts to the early DECnet model: no ARP or hop limit on ordinary point-to-point links, DDCMP for framing/reliability, NSP for connection-oriented task communication, network object numbers as port-like service selectors, DAP/FAL for remote files, MOP for maintenance/downline loading, and NCP for management.

The archive also records DDCMP data/control/maintenance frame structure and the Phase II NSP connection and initialization model, including CI/CC setup, `SEGSIZE`, ACK/NAK sequencing, flow control, Node Initialization and optional Node Verification. Its main relevance to GNet is historical: DECnet demonstrates a staged evolution from a reliable point-to-point link layer to connection-oriented services before general routed networking, without implying any change to current GNet protocol decisions.
